The case for change

Six pressures legacy campaign-finance systems leave teams to absorb.

Public reports identify recurring challenges in aging technology, filing quality, disclosure scale, and public access. MTX Elections Campaign Finance responds with reusable product patterns that agencies configure to their statutes, policies, and operating model.

01
⌁

Fragmented records and rekeying

Separate filing portals, inboxes, spreadsheets, and review systems make status difficult to follow and introduce repeat entry.

MTX responseA shared filer, committee, report, transaction, amendment, audit, and enforcement record.
02
⌛

Deadline-driven volume

Late-cycle surges place pressure on transaction ingestion, validation, review queues, notices, and public publishing.

MTX responseElastic electronic filing, guided queues, and deadline-aware processing.
03
△

Incomplete or inaccurate filings

Missing contributor details, inconsistent totals, and misclassified transactions can slow review and reduce the usefulness of disclosed data.

MTX responseInline guidance, transaction-level validation, and linked amendment workflows.
04
↻

Rules that change by cycle

Limits, forms, schedules, and review paths can change while hard-coded systems remain costly to modify.

MTX responseEffective-dated rules, forms, calendars, notices, and workflows.
05
◫

Opaque public search

Static reports and limited transaction search can hinder timely use by residents, media, and researchers.

MTX responseAccessible disclosure search, bulk downloads, visualizations, and open APIs.
06
◈

Aging technology risk

Unsupported components, scarce technical skills, and known vulnerabilities increase the burden of maintenance.

MTX responseModular cloud architecture with governed integration boundaries.

Assistive AI, bounded by authority

Support the work without transferring the decision.

MTX Elections Campaign Finance can use assistive AI to help classify supporting records, extract document text, identify missing contributor details, compare amendments, surface transaction anomalies, draft deficiency language, and suggest triage. Outputs are presented for review—not treated as agency decisions.

⚖

Human authority remains explicit. Authorized agency staff retain decision, approval, enforcement, and publication authority. Agencies define permitted uses, review steps, access, logging, and model governance.
